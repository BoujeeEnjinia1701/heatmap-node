# HeatMap Node STEM check

Verdict: the working principle of option A (a fan-aspirated radiation shield for air temperature and humidity, a 150 mm black globe for radiant heat, a cup anemometer for wind, and a computed outdoor WBGT) sits within known science, as does the working principle of option B (a directly measured natural wet-bulb temperature on a wetted wick with the same globe). Of 40 rows, **21 are within known science, 15 are plausible, to confirm with measurement, and 4 failed as first written, so a fix is proposed**. Three were fixed under Amish's standing instruction of 2026-10-23: the reservoir of option B splits when frozen if filled to the brim (K1), it runs dry in 7 days in a hot dry spell (K1b), and two nodes cannot resolve a 0.5 C or 1 C urban heat island difference from single readings (K2, a claim restated, not a design change). The fourth, S25 (the FieldNode cell charging in a 57.2 C interior), belongs to the FieldNode repo and goes to Amish with the fix proposed (HMN-DEC-001 O4). The independent review (below) also downgraded the shield claim S6 to plausible, which puts PR-001 at risk, and corrected the arm check (S9; a minimum yield of 110 MPa and the weight of the head added: factor 7.5 in place of 13.0). None of the fixes changes the working principle, the pitch or the scope. `stem_check` stays **open** because 15 rows can be closed only by measurement. Method: STANDARDS section 20. Calculations: `docs/04-calcs/stem_check.py`, which imports `docs/04-calcs/sizing.py` (HMN-CAL-001) and computes each row from its own law rather than copying the note's result.

## Fields not relevant

None. Every field is touched: physics (globe and wick energy balance, radiation, strength of materials, wind load, circuit loads), chemistry (corrosion, polymers, cell temperature limits, freezing), biology (heat stress screening index, insects and algae), mathematics (uncertainty and units) and engineering (sensing, radio and manufacturing).

## Concept options

- Option A, fan-aspirated shield with a globe and anemometer: the rows S1 to S30. Within known science in principle; the two unmeasured assumptions that matter most are the fan flow through the plate stack (S7) and the preload of the strap bands (S11).
- Option B, direct natural wet-bulb head: rows S31 to S37. Within known science in principle (a wetted wick in natural wind is the ISO 7243 reference measurement of the natural wet-bulb temperature); the details that failed were the freezing and capacity of the reservoir (fixed by K1 and K1b, both still plausible after the fix). The wick's delivery rate (S33), distilled-water upkeep (S35), mosquito and biofilm control (S36) and the 1.0 C accuracy of the measured WBGT (S37) are plausible, to confirm in the field.
